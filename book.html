Chapter 1

How to use this book · 8-week plan

Who this is for

You can already do A2 things: introduce yourself, shop, talk about your day in the present tense, form a basic Perfekt (Ich habe gearbeitet). You still mix up der/die/das, you freeze when the verb should go to the end, and you do not have enough words to tell a story. That is normal. B1 is the level where German stops being a phrasebook and becomes a language you can connect.

CEFR B1 in one sentence: you can handle most travel and everyday situations, talk about work, family, plans and opinions, and write a simple connected text. telc B1 tests exactly that — not poetry, not news-anchor German.

How to study (do this, not “read passively”)

  1. Read a grammar section once for understanding (English explanations are here on purpose).
  2. Copy the MEMORISE tables by hand into a notebook. Hand memory beats highlighting.
  3. Say every example aloud. B1 speaking rewards mouths that have already said the sentence.
  4. Learn vocabulary in chunks of 20–25 words, with article + plural. Never learn Tisch — learn der Tisch, die Tische.
  5. Write one letter every two days from Chapter 13, then check it against the model.
  6. Record yourself doing Sprechen Teil 2 for two minutes, three times a week.
  7. From week 4: timed reading and listening practice. Guessing is allowed. Blank answers are stupid — there is no penalty for wrong answers.
Do not Do not start with B2 YouTube. Do not collect ten apps. Do not only read. The exam is a performance: you must write a letter in 30 minutes and talk to a stranger in German for 15. Train that.

What “memorise” means here

You do not need 10,000 words. You need about 1,400–1,800 active words (with gender) plus 80 irregular verbs plus 40 connectors plus the letter and speaking formulas. That is this book. If a word is in a MEMORISE box or in Chapter 9, it is exam-useful. Learn those before rare synonyms.

Trick — the 80/20 of B1 Examiners reward clear, connected, appropriate language. A correct weil-clause and a polite Könnten Sie bitte…? beat a broken Konjunktiv I or a fancy word you cannot spell. Stay at clean B1.

Chapter 2

The telc Deutsch B1 exam

This chapter is the map of the test. Learn the map before you practise, or you will train the wrong muscle.

Two exams, two pass lines

telc Deutsch B1 is written + oral. You must pass both. Scoring well in reading cannot save a failed speaking exam, and the other way around.

Lesen Teil 1 — Globalverstehen (5 items)

Five short texts, ten headlines. Match the five correct headlines. The other five are traps: same topic, wrong focus, or extra information that is not in the text.

Trick Headlines use synonyms, not the same words as the text. Underline the core of each headline (job, rent, health, course, complaint). Then read for the main message of the whole text, not one sentence.

Lesen Teil 2 — Detailverstehen (5 items)

One longer article. Five multiple-choice questions (a / b / c). Answers are in order through the text. One option is often “almost right” except for a number, a negation, or a who/when swap.

Lesen Teil 3 — Selektives Lesen (10 items)

Ten people/situations and twelve short ads or notices (two ads are unused). Match who needs what. Scan for hard facts: price, time, age, location, “only evenings”, “no car needed”.

Time budget inside the 90 minutes Lesen 1: 15 min · Lesen 2: 20 min · Lesen 3: 20 min · Sprachbausteine 1: 12 min · Sprachbausteine 2: 12 min · Transfer answers to the answer sheet: 8–10 min. Never leave the answer sheet for the last 30 seconds.

Sprachbausteine Teil 1 — grammar cloze (10 items)

A short letter with 10 gaps. Each gap: a, b, or c. This is grammar in context: case, connector + word order, verb form, preposition, pronoun, Konjunktiv II, zu + infinitive.

Sprachbausteine Teil 2 — vocabulary bank (10 items)

A text with 10 gaps and about 15 words in a box. 10 fit, extras are distractors. You need collocations: eine Entscheidung treffen, sich bewerben um, im Vergleich zu.

Hören Teil 1 — five short texts, ONCE only (5 true/false)

Danger Teil 1 is played once. Read the statements before the audio. Listen for the one fact that makes it true or false (time, place, who, cancelled or not).

Hören Teil 2 — longer conversation, twice (10 true/false)

Interview or dialogue. Statements follow the order of the audio. The second listening is for the items you marked “?”.

Hören Teil 3 — short everyday scenes, twice (5 items)

Voicemails, announcements, shop dialogues. You need one specific piece of information. Read the statement first so your ear knows what to hunt.

Schreiben — 30 minutes, one text

You write a formal or informal email/letter answering a situation. There are four Leitpunkte (bullet points). You must cover all four in full sentences (not a shopping list). About 100–120 words. Register must match: Du to a friend, Sie to a company, landlord, course office, boss.

Zero rule If you write the wrong type of text, ignore the situation, or produce something unreadable, the whole writing paper can be scored 0. Always: greeting, four points, closing. Always Sie or du consistently.

Oral exam — 15 minutes + 20 minutes prep

Usually two candidates + two examiners. You get 20 minutes in a prep room with the task sheets for Teil 2 and Teil 3. You may take notes. You may not use a dictionary or phone. Teil 1 is not prepared — it is live small talk.

PartWhat you doTime

Exam-day kit and behaviour

  • Passport or national ID (the name must match the registration).
  • HB/2B pencils, eraser, pen for the letter if the centre wants ink — follow the invigilator.
  • Watch without smart functions if phones must be outside.

Trick — numbers in listening dreizehn (13) vs dreißig (30): the -zig ending is “tsich”. sechzehn / sechzig, siebzehn / siebzig are the classic traps. Write digits during Hören.

Spelling rules you need for the letter

  • Nouns are capitalised. Always. die Wohnung, das Problem, der Termin.
  • ß vs ss: after a long vowel or diphthong: ß (Fuß, weiß, stoßen). After a short vowel: ss (müssen, dass, Schloss). Swiss centres may use ss only — follow local convention; in Germany use ß.
  • dass vs das: dass = “that” as a connector (verb at the end). das = the/that/which (article, pronoun, relative for neuter).
  • mal vs Mal: einmal often one word. das erste Mal — noun, capital.
  • Compound nouns are one word: der Krankenversicherungsschein looks scary; split it in your head: Kranken + Versicherungs + Schein.
  • Umlaut in plural often: der Vater → die Väter, die Stadt → die Städte.

Chapter 4

Nouns, gender, plural, and the four cases

German meaning lives in endings, not in word order alone. If you get case wrong, the sentence can still be understood — but Sprachbausteine will mark it wrong, and writing accuracy drops. This chapter is non-negotiable.

Every noun has a gender. Learn it with the word.

Three genders: masculine der, feminine die, neuter das. In the plural, all nouns take die in the nominative/accusative.

Memorise — gender patterns that are reliable enough

Usually masculine (der): days, months, seasons, weather, car brands, alcoholic drinks (except das Bier), nouns in -ling, -ig, -ich, -ismus, -ner, -er for male people/jobs (Lehrer, Fahrer).

Usually feminine (die): -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -tion/-sion, -tät, -ik, -ei, -in (female job), -enz, most trees and flowers, numbers used as nouns (die Eins).

Usually neuter (das): -chen, -lein (Mädchen, Häuschen), -ment, -um, -tum (often), infinitives used as nouns (das Schwimmen), colours, metals, hotels/cinemas used as nouns, diminutives, most ge- collectives (das Gebäude).

Exceptions you will meet: das Restaurant, das Problem, das Thema, das Foto, das Auto, die Butter, die Kartoffel, der Käse, der Löffel, das Messer, die Gabel, der Honig, die Milch, das Obst, das Gemüse, der Nachtisch.

n-Deklination (weak masculine nouns)

A group of masculine nouns add -n or -en in every case except nominative singular. You must recognise them in Sprachbausteine.

Typical: male beings and animals in -e (der Junge, der Kollege, der Kunde, der Experte, der Löwe), and der Mensch, der Nachbar, der Herr, der Student, der Präsident, der Patient, der Journalist, der Elefant, plus der Name, der Gedanke, der Glaube (these also take -ns in genitive: des Namens).

Ich kenne den Kunden. Ich helfe dem Kunden. die Idee des Kunden.

Herr adds -n: Guten Tag, Herrn Müller (on envelopes: Herrn).

Proper names and articles

People usually have no article: Anna kommt. Ich sehe Anna. In southern speech you hear der Thomas — do not write that in the exam. Countries mostly no article: in Deutschland, nach Spanien. Exceptions with article: die Schweiz, die Türkei, die USA, der Iran, der Irak, der Sudan — in die Schweiz, in der Schweiz.

Comparative and superlative

Comparative: -er (+ umlaut on short adjectives: älter, jünger, größer, länger, stärker, kürzer). Superlative: am -sten or der/die/das -ste.

Berlin ist größer als Bonn. München ist am teuersten. Das ist die beste Lösung.

Irregular: gut – besser – am besten; viel – mehr – am meisten; gern – lieber – am liebsten; hoch – höher – am höchsten; nah – näher – am nächsten.

immer + comparative = more and more: immer teurer, immer schwieriger.

je … desto / je … umso: Je mehr ich lerne, desto sicherer werde ich. (verb last in the je-clause, verb second after desto).

Chapter 7

Word order, connectors, relative clauses

B1 is the level of connected text. Examiners listen for verbs in the right place. If you only chain und-sentences, you sound A2. If you use weil, dass, obwohl, wenn, deshalb correctly, you sound B1 even with simple words.

The iron rules

  1. In a normal statement the conjugated verb is in position 2. Position 1 is one unit (a word or a phrase): Heute | fahre | ich nach Hause. Um 8 Uhr | beginnt | der Kurs. Meine Schwester | wohnt | in Köln.
  2. Yes/no question: verb first: Kommst du mit?
  3. W-question: question word, then verb: Wann beginnt der Kurs?
  4. Subordinate clause: conjunction first, conjugated verb last: …, weil der Kurs um 8 Uhr beginnt.
  5. If a subordinate clause comes first, the main clause starts with the verb (still “verb second” of the whole sentence): Weil ich krank bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.
  6. Participles and infinitives wait at the end: Ich habe gestern einen Brief geschrieben. Ich will morgen Deutsch lernen.
The classic error After weil / dass / wenn / obwohl candidates put the verb in position 2 like English. Wrong: *weil ich bin krank. Right: weil ich krank bin.

What counts as “position 1”

One idea: time, place, object, or a whole subordinate clause. Not two ideas. Wrong: *Heute in Berlin fahre ich… Right: Heute fahre ich nach Berlin. or Nach Berlin fahre ich heute.

Time–Manner–Place is a useful default for the rest of the middle field: Ich fahre morgen mit dem Zug nach Hamburg. (wann – wie – wohin).

Chapter 10

Lesen — reading that scores

You have 90 minutes for reading and Sprachbausteine. Reading is 20 items. You do not need to understand every word. You need the task type.

Universal reading method

  1. Read the instruction (what to match, how many extras).
  2. Read the questions or headlines first, not the long text. Your brain then hunts.
  3. Underline names, numbers, negations, times, must/only/not.
  4. When you find an answer, mark it and move. Do not fall in love with one sentence.
  5. Unknown word: look at the sentence around it. Is it a thing, a person, a verb? Often you do not need it.
  6. Last 8 minutes of the 90: copy every answer onto the Antwortbogen.
Synonym radar telc almost never repeats the headline word in the text. Train this translation in your head:

job = Stelle / Beruf / arbeiten / Tätigkeit · cheap = günstig / preiswert / nicht teuer · free time = Freizeit / neben der Arbeit / nach Feierabend · health = Gesundheit / sich fit halten / Krankheit vermeiden · course = Kurs / Unterricht / Lehrgang · rent = Miete / Wohnung · complain = sich beschweren / unzufrieden · only evenings = nur abends / nach 18 Uhr / berufsbegleitend.

Teil 1 — five texts, ten headlines (5 answers)

Five short newspaper-style paragraphs. Ten titles. Five titles are wrong: they are too narrow, too extreme, or add a fact that is not there.

Method

  1. Read all ten headlines. Write a one-word topic next to each (Job, Miete, Sport…).
  2. Read text A. Ask: what is the whole text doing? Warning? Offer? Personal story? Result of a study?
  3. Pick the headline that summarises all of it. If two seem possible, the wrong one usually contains a word that is slightly off (always vs sometimes, free vs cheap, children vs adults).
  4. Cross out used headlines. Never reuse.
Mini example

Text: Immer mehr Firmen erlauben ihren Mitarbeitern, zwei Tage pro Woche zu Hause zu arbeiten. Viele Beschäftigte sind zufrieden, weil sie Zeit sparen. Manche Chefs haben aber Angst, dass die Arbeit leidet. Eine Studie zeigt: Die Produktivität bleibt gleich, wenn klare Regeln existieren.

Teil 2 — one long text, five a/b/c questions

Questions follow the text order. Each question has a paragraph. Read question 1, scan until you have it, then question 2.

  • Number swap: text says 15 hours, option says 50.

Chapter 11

Sprachbausteine — the 30 quiet points

This subtest is short and extremely learnable. It is grammar (Teil 1) and collocation vocabulary (Teil 2). Candidates who “only speak” leak points here. Candidates who memorised Chapters 4–8 pick up easy marks.

Teil 1 — letter with 10 gaps, a/b/c

Always a semi-formal or informal email. Read the whole letter once for sense (who writes to whom? du or Sie?). Then one gap at a time.

What they test (in order of frequency)

  1. Connector + word order partner (weil / denn / deshalb / obwohl / dass / wenn / als / damit / um).
  2. Case after preposition or verb (für den, mit der, dem Nachbarn helfen).
  3. Verb form: Perfekt auxiliary, Partizip, modal + infinitive, Konjunktiv II (könnten, würde, hätte).
  4. Pronoun (ihn/ihm, sie/ihr, darauf, worüber).
  5. Adjective ending (ein neues, dem neuen, bei gutem Wetter).
  6. zu + infinitive vs bare infinitive.
  7. Relative pronoun (der/den/dem/das/die/denen).
  8. Particles: schon, noch, erst, nur, etwas, ein bisschen.
Elimination trick If two options are the same meaning but different grammar, the meaning is not the test — the ending is. If options are weil / denn / deshalb, check verb position in the sentence that follows the gap. The option that fits the already written word order is right.
Worked mini-cloze

1 a) denn   b) weil   c) deshalb   → The verb is already at the end (muss). So weil (b). denn would need ich muss am Abend arbeiten. deshalb would start a new sentence with verb second.

2 a) habe   b) bin   c) würde   → sich freuen takes haben. habe (a). Plus the participle gefreut.

3 a) Können   b) Könnten   c) Müssen   → Polite suggestion: Könnten (b).

4 a) jederzeit   b) keinem   c) den   → Meaning: you can call me any time. jederzeit (a).

Chapter 12

Hören — listening under exam rules

You cannot “read” your way to listening points. You can still prepare the task so the audio has fewer places to hide. This chapter is method + transcripts you must read aloud (or have a friend read) until the patterns are in your ear. Then sit the official telc MP3 mock with a timer.

Before any recording starts

  1. Read every statement. Underline the word that will decide true vs false: a time, a price, a person, nicht / nur / schon / erst / nicht mehr.
  2. Predict the situation: announcement, voicemail, interview, shop?
  3. Write a tiny symbol: ? next to items you will listen for extra hard.

Chapter 13

Schreiben — the 30-minute letter

This is the most “template-able” part of telc B1. Examiners do not want a novel. They want a complete, appropriate, connected email that hits four Leitpunkte. Memorise the skeletons in this chapter until you can write them with the clock covered.

What you will be asked to write

A reply to a situation: invitation, information request, complaint, appointment change, course office, landlord, neighbour, colleague, friend. Register is either informal (du) or formal (Sie). The task tells you who the person is. Friend/family → du. Company, Amt, unknown adult, landlord, course leader you don’t know well → Sie.

ClockDo this
0–3 minCircle Sie or du. Number the four points. Decide opening and closing. Jot one idea per point.
3–24 minWrite. One short paragraph per Leitpunkt. Connect with weil/deshalb/leider/trotzdem.
24–30 minCount words (100–120 is the sweet spot; 80 is short, 160 is risky). Check: all four points? Greeting and close? Verb last after weil? Capital nouns? Sie/du consistent?
Automatic damage Missing a Leitpunkt costs content points. Keyword lists without sentences look A2. Mixing du and Sie looks careless. No greeting/no closing hurts communicative score. Wrong situation (you write a story about your holiday when they asked you to complain about a washing machine) can zero the paper.

The four-point rule

Each Leitpunkt needs at least two connected clauses (or one rich sentence with a weil/dass). Not: Termin: Dienstag. Yes: Leider kann ich am Montag nicht, weil ich bis 18 Uhr arbeiten muss. Könnten wir den Termin auf Dienstag um 19 Uhr verschieben?

If a point asks for a suggestion, give a concrete one (day, time, place, amount). Vague irgendwann is weak.

Chapter 14

Sprechen — 15 minutes that decide the certificate

The oral exam is a pair performance. Examiners score whether you can contact, give an opinion with an example, and plan something together. Perfect grammar is nice. Dead air and monologues are expensive. Train with a human if you can; if not, record both roles on your phone.

The 20-minute prep

You receive the sheets for Teil 2 and Teil 3. Teil 1 is not on the sheet — it is live.

  • Teil 2: write 6–8 bullet points, not a speech. Keywords: opinion, two reasons, one personal example, one advantage, one disadvantage, a question for your partner.
  • Teil 3: for each Leitpunkt on the planning sheet, write one suggestion + one reason. Add a backup suggestion. Star the points you will open with.
  • Do not write full sentences you will read. Reading aloud scores badly and kills eye contact.
  • If you don’t know a word, write a bypass: so etwas wie… / ein Ding zum…
Partner strategy If your partner is stronger, do not hide. Ask them questions. If they are weaker, invite them in: Was meinst du? / Hast du eine andere Idee? If they freeze, offer a choice: Sollen wir am Samstag oder am Sonntag treffen? Helping is not cheating; it is Aufgabenbewältigung.

Teil 1 — Kontaktaufnahme (~2–3 minutes)

Greet the partner (not only the examiners). Ask two or three real follow-ups. Do not interview them like the police. React: Ah, interessant. Ich auch… / Wirklich? Wie lange schon?

Your 40-second intro (customise and memorise)

Hallo, ich heiße … Ich komme aus … und wohne seit … in … Ich arbeite als … / Ich mache eine Ausbildung / Ich besuche einen Deutschkurs, weil ich die B1-Prüfung machen möchte. In meiner Freizeit … Und du? Wohnst du schon lange hier?

Question bank (pick 4 and rotate):

  • Woher kommst du? Seit wann lernst du Deutsch?
  • Wohnst du in einer Wohnung oder in einem Haus? Allein oder mit Familie?
  • Was machst du beruflich? Gefällt dir die Arbeit?
  • Was machst du gerne am Wochenende?
  • Hast du Familie hier? Fehlt dir etwas aus deiner Heimat?
  • Warum machst du diese Prüfung?

If they already said they work in a hospital, do not ask “do you work?”. Ask Welche Schichten hast du? Ist das anstrengend?

Teil 2 — talk about a topic (~4–5 minutes)

You get a prompt: a statement, a photo, a short quote. Structure beats brilliance.

The 90-second spine
  1. Say what the topic is: Hier geht es um… / Das Thema ist…
  2. Opinion: Meiner Meinung nach… / Ich finde, dass… / Ich glaube, dass…
  3. Reason: …, weil… / Der Grund ist, dass…
  4. Example from your life: Zum Beispiel in meiner Familie… / Letztes Jahr habe ich…
  5. The other side: Allerdings gibt es auch Nachteile. Zum Beispiel…
  6. Close + ball to partner: Deshalb würde ich sagen, dass… Und du? Siehst du das auch so?

Then listen. React before your next idea: Da hast du recht. / Das sehe ich etwas anders, weil… / Interessant, das kenne ich auch.

Pronunciation and fluency (what examiners actually need)

  • Speak a bit slower than you think. Clarity > speed.
  • Finish your sentences. A simple finished weil-clause beats a complex crash.
  • If you err, correct once: Entschuldigung, ich meine… Then continue. Do not apologise five times.
  • Fillers that still sound German: also, na ja, ich würde sagen, Moment mal — not eeeh for ten seconds.

B. Lesen Teil 2

Mehr als 40 Prozent der Deutschen leben allein — Tendenz steigend. Das hat Vorteile: man entscheidet frei über Alltag und Einrichtung. Gleichzeitig berichten viele Alleinlebende, dass sie abends selten Besuch haben und im Krankheitsfall niemanden im Haus finden. In großen Städten sind Singlewohnungen teuer, deshalb teilen sich jüngere Leute oft eine WG, ohne wirklich „zusammenzuleben“. Vereine und Nachbarschaftscafés versuchen, Begegnung zu organisieren. Experten sagen: Einsamkeit ist nicht dasselbe wie allein sein. Man kann eine Wohnung allein haben und trotzdem ein dichtes soziales Netz. Wer neu in einer Stadt ist, sollte aktiv werden: einen Kurs, einen Sportverein oder ehrenamtliche Arbeit. Digitale Chats helfen ein bisschen, ersetzen aber das echte Treffen nicht, vor allem nicht für ältere Menschen, die das Internet unsicher finden.
