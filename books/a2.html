Chapter 1

How to use this book · 8-week plan

Who this is for

You can already do A1 survival: say who you are, order in a café, ask the price, talk about your day in the present tense, and write a short form. You still freeze when the story moves to yesterday. You say Ich gehe Bahnhof instead of Ich gehe zum Bahnhof. You know weil means “because” but you leave the verb in the English place. That is normal. A2 is the level where German starts to connect: past + reason + place.

CEFR A2 in one sentence: you can handle simple everyday tasks, describe your background and surroundings in simple terms, and write a short connected message about familiar things. telc Deutsch A2 tests exactly that — not B1 complaint letters, not Konjunktiv II production, not long relative-clause essays.

How to study (do this, not “read passively”)

  1. Read a grammar section once for understanding (English explanations are here on purpose).
  2. Copy the MEMORISE tables by hand into a notebook. Hand memory beats highlighting.
  3. Say every example aloud. A2 speaking rewards mouths that have already said the sentence.
  4. Learn vocabulary as article + noun. Never learn Wohnung — learn die Wohnung. Never learn Arzt — learn der Arzt.
  5. Write ten true Perfekt sentences about yesterday every day in weeks 1–2. Then write one short message every two days from Chapter 10.
  6. Record yourself doing the Teil 1 intro and one Teil 2 topic, three times a week from week 4.
  7. From week 5: timed reading and listening on the website. Guessing is allowed. Blank answers are wasted — there is no penalty for wrong answers.
Do not Do not start with B1 YouTube “Konjunktiv II in 10 minutes”. Do not collect ten apps. Do not only read. The exam is a performance: you must write a connected message in about 30 minutes and talk to a stranger in German for about 15. Train that.

Chapter 2

The telc Deutsch A2 exam

This chapter is the map of the test. Learn the map before you practise, or you will train the wrong muscle — for example a B1 100-word formal complaint, or a 20-minute speaking prep that A2 usually does not give you.

60 points, pass typically 60%

telc Deutsch A2 / Start Deutsch 2 is a 60-point exam: Hören 15, Lesen 15, Schreiben 15, Sprechen 15. Pass is typically 60% overall (36/60). Confirm at your centre whether they also want a pass in each half. This is not the DTZ A2·B1 exam and not telc B1 (no 90-minute Sprachbausteine, no 225-point written paper).

Budget writing inside that shared 50 minutes (about 20 minutes to write). A perfect Lesen sheet and five lines of Schreiben is a bad trade. If you get two writing tasks, write both; keep the short one short.

Hören — about 20 minutes, three parts

Typical pattern: short public announcements or voicemails; then a longer conversation or interview; then several short everyday scenes (shop, phone, station). Teil 1 is often played once. Later parts are usually played twice. Read every statement in the pause before the audio. Hunt the fact: a time, a price, a name, nicht / nur / schon / erst.

Danger — Teil 1 once If you freeze, you lose the item. Do not try to understand every word. Catch the one fact the statement is about. Then let the rest go. There is a short pause between texts — use it to look at the next statement, not to mourn the last one.

Lesen — same 50-minute booklet as Schreiben

You will see the furniture of everyday Germany: a notice on a door, a short email from a course office, a classified ad for a flat or a bike, a supermarket flyer. Tasks are matching (who needs what / which headline fits) or multiple choice. You do not need every word. You need the question first, then synonyms (Chapter 8).

Time budget inside the 50 minutes (Lesen + Schreiben) Lesen signs/ads/text: about 25–28 min · Transfer Lesen answers: 4 min · Schreiben: about 18–20 min. Never leave the answer sheet or the letter for the last 20 seconds.

Schreiben — inside the same 50 minutes

You write a message or short letter answering a situation. There are Leitpunkte (guiding bullets). You must cover all of them in full sentences — not a shopping list. About 60–80 words is the A2 sweet spot. Register must match: du to a friend, Sie to a course office, landlord, shop, or unknown adult.

Zero-risk rules Missing a Leitpunkt costs content. Mixing du and Sie looks careless. No greeting and no closing hurts. Wrong situation (you write about your holiday when they asked you to change an appointment) can sink the paper. Always: Anrede, all points, Gruß.

No B1-style Sprachbausteine block

Adult telc Deutsch A2 does not have a separate 90-minute Sprachbausteine paper like Zertifikat Deutsch B1. Grammar is folded into Lesen (fitting word in context) and Schreiben (you must produce correct forms). The website may still offer extra gap drills — use them as practice, not as “the A2 exam”. Do not skip weil / dass because there is no SB booklet.

Oral exam — about 15 minutes, usually a pair

Usually two candidates + two examiners. Unlike telc B1, you typically get no 20-minute preparation room. Teil 1 is live small talk. Teil 2 and Teil 3 prompts are in front of you when you sit down. You may glance at the sheet; you may not read a speech. No dictionary, no phone.

Always answer There is no minus marking. A random a/b/c or R/F is better than a blank. For matching, use remaining unused options by elimination. For Schreiben, a short complete message beats a perfect first paragraph and nothing else.

Chapter 3

Perfekt — the spoken past you must own

This is the A2 tense. In the exam you will tell what you did last weekend, why you missed a course, and what you bought. The formula is small: conjugated haben or sein in position 2 + Partizip II at the end. Master the decision and forty participles, and half of Schreiben and Sprechen becomes easy.

The frame

Ich habe gestern Pizza gegessen. · Ich bin um 8 Uhr aufgestanden. · Wir sind nach Köln gefahren.

Time words like gestern, am Wochenende, letzte Woche, vor zwei Tagen sit comfortably after the auxiliary or in position 1: Gestern habe ich gearbeitet.

haben vs sein — the decision

AuxiliaryWhenExamples
habenMost verbs. Anything with a direct object. Thinking, eating, buying, seeing, helping, working, sleeping.Ich habe gearbeitet. Ich habe das Auto gefahren. Ich habe Pizza gegessen.
seinMotion from A to B with no object. Change of state. Plus the three stay/be/become verbs: bleiben, sein, werden.Ich bin gegangen / gefahren / geflogen / gekommen. Ich bin aufgestanden. Ich bin eingeschlafen. Ich bin zu Hause geblieben. Ich bin krank gewesen.
Trick — ask two questions 1) Does the person move or change (go, come, fly, wake, fall asleep, become)? And 2) is there no thing being driven/eaten/seen? If both yes → sein. If you drive the car (object) → haben: Ich habe das Auto gefahren vs Ich bin nach Berlin gefahren.
Never Never ich habe gegangen, ich habe gekommen, ich habe gefahren (when you mean you travelled). Motion without an object takes sein: ich bin gegangen / gekommen / gefahren.

No ge- list — memorise Prefixes be-, ver-, er-, ent-, emp-, miss-, zer- (when unstressed) never take an extra ge- in the participle: besucht, verloren, erzählt, entschieden, empfohlen. Chapter 4

Cases: nominative, accusative, dative

A1 gave you “the / a” in the nominative and a first look at den / einen. A2 lives in the dative. If you can say Ich fahre mit dem Bus zum Arzt and Das Kleid gefällt mir, you sound like someone who belongs in the exam room.

You do not need a full genitive production system for telc A2. Recognise wegen des Wetters in Lesen if it appears; say wegen dem Wetter in speech if you must — examiners care more about dative after mit and helfen.

Chapter 5

Connectors and word order

A2 is the level of connected text. Examiners listen for verbs in the right place. If you only chain und-sentences, you still sound A1. If you use weil, dass, wenn, deshalb correctly, you sound A2 even with simple words. You do not need a B1 dump of obwohl, damit, nachdem as production tools. You need four verb-last words and three verb-second words — cold.

The iron rules

  1. In a normal statement the conjugated verb is in position 2. Position 1 is one unit: Heute | fahre | ich nach Hause. Um 8 Uhr | beginnt | der Kurs.
  2. Yes/no question: verb first: Kommst du mit?
  3. W-question: question word, then verb: Wann beginnt der Kurs?
  4. Subordinate clause: conjunction first, conjugated verb last: …, weil der Kurs um 8 Uhr beginnt.
  5. If a subordinate clause comes first, the main clause starts with the verb: Weil ich krank bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.
  6. Participles and infinitives wait at the end: Ich habe gestern einen Brief geschrieben. Ich will morgen Deutsch lernen.
The classic error After weil / dass / wenn / als candidates put the verb in position 2 like English. Wrong: *weil ich bin krank. Right: weil ich krank bin.

TMP — Time, Manner, Place

TMP tip A helpful default for the middle of the sentence, not a prison: wann – wie – wo/wohin. Ich fahre morgen mit dem Zug nach Hamburg. (time – manner – place). Separable verbs + connectors

In a main clause the prefix goes to the end: Ich stehe um 7 Uhr auf. In a weil-clause the whole verb sits last, prefix attached: …, weil ich um 7 Uhr aufstehe. Perfekt: …, weil ich um 7 Uhr aufgestanden bin.

Schreiben insurance One weil (verb last) + one Perfekt + one deshalb or dann is an A2 letter that looks planned. Tick them in the margin before you hand in.

Chapter 8

Lesen — reading that scores

You have about 30 minutes for reading. You do not need to understand every word. You need the task type and a synonym radar. A2 texts are notices on doors, short emails, ads, and simple articles about everyday life in Germany. The grammar is A2; the trap is usually a number, a nicht, or a word that looks like the question but means something else.

Universal method — question first

  1. Read the instruction (match? choose a/b/c? how many extras?).
  2. Read the questions, headlines, or people first, not the long text. Your brain then hunts.
  3. Underline names, numbers, negations, times, only/must/not.
  4. When you find an answer, mark it and move. Do not fall in love with one sentence.
  5. Unknown word: look at the sentence around it. Is it a thing, a person, a verb? Often you do not need it.
  6. Last 4 minutes: copy every answer onto the Antwortbogen.

Chapter 9

Hören — listening under exam rules

You cannot “read” your way to listening points. You can still prepare the task so the audio has fewer places to hide. This chapter is method + original announcements you must read aloud (or have a friend read) until the patterns are in your ear. Then sit the website Hören papers in exam mode, and in weeks 7–8 the official telc sample MP3s.

Before any recording starts

  1. Read every statement. Underline the word that will decide true vs false: a time, a price, a person, nicht / nur / schon / erst / nicht mehr.
  2. Predict the situation: announcement, voicemail, shop, station?
  3. Write a tiny ? next to items you will listen for extra hard.
  4. Pencil on the question paper first; copy to the Antwortbogen after the last part.
Teil 1 is often played once If you freeze, you lose the item. Do not try to understand every word. Catch the one fact the statement is about. Then let the rest go. There is a short pause between the texts — use it to look at the next statement, not to mourn the last one.

Later parts are usually played twice. First pass: answer the obvious ones. Second pass: only the remaining. If still unsure, guess before the next part begins. No minus marking.

Chapter 10

Schreiben — the 60–80 word message

This is the most “template-able” part of telc A2. Examiners do not want a novel and they do not want a B1 100-word formal complaint as your main trick. They want a complete, appropriate, connected message that hits every Leitpunkt. Memorise the skeletons until you can write them with the clock covered.

What you will be asked to write

A reply to a situation: invitation, appointment change, course office, landlord, shop, neighbour, friend. Some packs print two short tasks; some print one letter with three or four bullets. Train one connected 60–80 word text that covers all points. If the day gives you two tasks, write both; keep the tiny one tiny.

Register is either informal (du) or semi-formal / formal (Sie). The task tells you who the person is. Friend/family → du. Course office, shop, unknown adult, landlord → Sie.

Chapter 11

Sprechen — 15 minutes in a pair

The oral exam is a pair performance. Examiners score whether you can contact, say something about a topic with a reason, and plan something together. Perfect grammar is nice. Dead air and monologues are expensive. Unlike telc B1, you typically get no 20-minute prep room. Train so the scripts are already in your mouth.

Train with a human if you can; if not, record both roles on your phone.

Partner strategy If your partner is stronger, do not hide. Ask them questions. If they are weaker, invite them in: Was meinst du? / Hast du eine andere Idee? If they freeze, offer a choice: Sollen wir uns am Samstag oder am Sonntag treffen? Helping is not cheating; it is the task.

Teil 1 — Kontaktaufnahme (~3–4 minutes)

Greet the partner (not only the examiners). Ask two or three real follow-ups. Do not interview them like the police. React: Ah, interessant. Ich auch… / Wirklich? Wie lange schon?

Your intro — customise and memorise (Freizeit + weil)

Hallo, ich heiße … Ich komme aus … und wohne seit … in … Ich arbeite als … / Ich mache eine Ausbildung / Ich besuche einen Deutschkurs, weil ich die A2-Prüfung machen möchte. In meiner Freizeit koche ich gern und treffe Freunde, weil ich nach der Arbeit entspannen möchte. Und du? Wohnst du schon lange hier?

The weil is not decoration. It is the A2 signal: you can connect. Swap the free-time facts for yours, but keep the reason.

Question bank (pick 4 and rotate):

  • Woher kommst du? Seit wann lernst du Deutsch?
  • Wohnst du in einer Wohnung oder in einem Haus? Allein oder mit Familie?
  • Was machst du beruflich? Gefällt dir die Arbeit?
  • Was machst du gerne am Wochenende?
  • Hast du Familie hier?
  • Warum machst du diese Prüfung?

If they already said they work in a hospital, do not ask “do you work?”. Ask Welche Schichten hast du? Ist das anstrengend?

Teil 2 — talk about a topic (~4–5 minutes)

You get a photo or a simple prompt: free time, city vs village, shopping, sport, learning German, food. Structure beats brilliance. You are not giving a B1 advantages/disadvantages lecture. You are saying what you see or think, why, and one example from your life — then you pass the ball.

The Teil 2 spine
  1. Name it: Hier geht es um… / Auf dem Foto sehe ich… / Das Thema ist…
  2. Opinion: Ich finde, dass… / Meiner Meinung nach… / Ich glaube, dass…
  3. Reason: …, weil…
  4. Example: Zum Beispiel in meiner Freizeit… / Letztes Wochenende habe ich…
  5. A small other side (optional): Aber manchmal… / Allerdings ist das auch teuer.
  6. Ball: Und du? Was denkst du? / Machst du das auch?

Then listen. React before your next idea: Da hast du recht. / Das sehe ich etwas anders, weil… / Interessant, das kenne ich auch.

Why these five hurt most

Perfekt auxiliary. Ich habe gegangen is the A2 neon sign. Motion without an object = sein. Drill ten sentences a night until your hand refuses the wrong auxiliary.

Verb last after weil. English order after weil is the second neon sign. Say the chant: weil + rest + VERB.

Ich gehe Bahnhof. German wants a preposition and a case: zum Bahnhof, zur Arbeit, zum Arzt, in die Stadt, nach Hause.

du/Sie mix. One slip can be nerves. A whole paragraph of mix is register failure. Circle the column before you write word one.

Ich habe 25 Jahre. Age uses sein: Ich bin 25 Jahre alt. You have a brother, a car, hunger — not years of age.

  • dass connector vs das article.
  • Perfekt: ge- on the participle unless inseparable or -ieren.
  • Umlaut on möchte, für, Tür, Grüße, spät, können.

Exam-day card

  • Passport or national ID — name matches the registration.
  • Pencils (HB/2B), eraser, pen if the centre wants ink for Schreiben.
  • Watch without smart functions if phones stay outside.
  • Arrive early. Toilets before Hören. Water if allowed.
  • Hören: read the statement first. Teil 1 often once. Hunt numbers / nicht / nur.
  • Lesen: questions first. Synonyms. Transfer to the Antwortbogen with minutes left.
  • Schreiben: circle du or Sie. Tick every Leitpunkt. 60–80 words. Greeting + close. One weil, one Perfekt if the story is past.
  • Sprechen: greet the partner. Intro + Freizeit + weil. Teil 2 spine. Teil 3: agree out loud.
  • Always answer. No minus marking.
  • If you miss an instruction: Könnten Sie das bitte wiederholen?
